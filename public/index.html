What you'd ask before letting an AI near your network

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The model, the data, the logs and every answer stay on your server. Nothing is sent to an AI service, or to us.

What access does it need?

A dedicated read-only account for each system. You create the accounts, and you can see and revoke each one.

Can it change anything?

No. It recommends, drafts tickets and writes the exact commands; a person runs them.

What if it makes something up?

Code checks each concrete claim against what the tools returned. A claim that isn't in the data is corrected or marked "not confirmed".

Who can use it?

Your own directory accounts, limited to a group you choose. Roles can give managers and help-desk staff different tools and data.

What hardware?

One server or workstation with one NVIDIA GPU. A 12 GB card runs today's model; 16 GB is a good start.

Things it found that nobody was looking for

A log that went quiet

A group of servers had stopped sending a whole category of Windows logs for weeks. No alert fires on silence. Missing data is now flagged the day it stops.

An audit setting that switched itself off

Two kinds of audit policy were overriding each other, turning sign-in auditing off and on every few minutes. The review named both; one was removed.

A permission nobody remembered granting

An account could copy every password hash in the domain. The admin removed the right, asked "did it work?", and the check re-ran live and confirmed it.

One phone, three addresses

Guest Wi-Fi sessions to a known command-and-control server looked like three machines. Lining up the times pointed to one phone, which was blocked.

Reports that hid their own names

A tenant setting anonymised the cloud usage reports. The review refused to guess and named the setting to change.

Questions we get asked

Is it air-gapped?

The model and your data never leave your network. IT Sentinel does make outbound read calls to the cloud services you connect (Microsoft 365, a cloud EDR) and to threat-intelligence feeds. Leave those out and it needs no internet at all.

Which AI model does it use?

An open-weight model that runs locally; the exact model depends on your hardware.

How fast is it?

Most answers take a few seconds. A long plan or a full investigation can take around half a minute.

Does it work in languages other than English?

It answers in the language of the question. English and Hebrew are in daily use today.

We already have an outsourced SOC. Does this replace it?

No, it works next to it. It can forward the logs your SOC needs in real time, and it answers the questions a SOC doesn't.

Is it a product or a service?

Both: the software plus the work of connecting and tuning it.
