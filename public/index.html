Security questions, answered first

What you'd ask before letting an AI near your network

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The model, the data, the logs and every answer stay on your server. No prompts or results are sent to an AI service, or to us.

What access does it need?

A dedicated read-only account for each system it connects to. You create the accounts, and you can see and revoke each one.

Can it change anything?

No. Its tools only read. It recommends, drafts tickets and writes the exact commands; a person runs them. The one thing it writes is a help-desk ticket, when you ask for one.

What if it makes something up?

Code checks each concrete claim against what the tools returned. A claim that isn't in the data gets one correction round, then is marked "not confirmed". If a data source is down, the answer says so.

Who can use it?

Sign-in uses your own directory accounts, limited to a group you choose. Roles can give managers and help-desk staff different tools and data.

What if it stops?

A watchdog checks the SIEM, the model and its GPU, and posts to Teams when one of them stops. A quiet assistant is never mistaken for a quiet network.

Things it found that nobody was looking for

Five findings from real networks

Details are removed on purpose. We don't name customers, and we don't put numbers on results we can't show you.

A log that went quiet

The problem

A group of servers had stopped sending a whole category of Windows logs, for weeks. Every dashboard looked fine, because no alert fires on silence.

How it surfaced

The daily "questions worth asking" compares what should arrive with what did, and named the missing log category.

What changed

Logging was restored, and missing data is now flagged the day it stops.

An audit setting that switched itself off

The problem

Two kinds of audit policy were overriding each other, turning sign-in auditing off and on every few minutes.

How it surfaced

As a flood of one event. The review traced it to the two policies and named both.

What changed

One policy was removed. The IT review now checks every machine for this, and tells it apart from a harmless Windows refresh.

A permission nobody remembered granting

The problem

An account held the right to copy every password hash in the domain (DCSync).

How it surfaced

The scheduled directory audit found it. Investigate explained the risk and the exact fix.

What changed

The admin removed the right, asked "did it work?", and the check re-ran live and confirmed it.

One phone, three addresses

The problem

Firewall sessions from the guest Wi-Fi to a known command-and-control server, from what looked like three machines.

How it surfaced

The threat-intelligence feed flagged the sessions. Lining the times up with the connected clients pointed to one phone moving between access points.

What changed

The phone was blocked, and the feed has shown no attempts since.

Reports that hid their own names

The problem

A tenant setting anonymised the cloud usage reports, which would have turned one departed user's files into dozens.

How it surfaced

The Microsoft 365 review noticed the names were hidden and refused to guess.

What changed

The report names the setting to change, so the clean-up list is right the first time.

FAQ

Questions we get asked

Is it air-gapped?

The model and your data never leave your network. IT Sentinel does make outbound read calls to the cloud services you connect (Microsoft 365, a cloud EDR) and to threat-intelligence feeds, as your admins already do. Leave those out and it needs no internet at all.

Which AI model does it use?

An open-weight model that runs locally; the exact model depends on your hardware.

How fast is it?

Most answers take a few seconds. A long plan or a full investigation can take around half a minute.

Can it make changes?

No. It's read-only. It drafts tickets and exact steps for a person.

Does it work in languages other than English?

It answers in the language of the question. English and Hebrew are in daily use today.

We already have an outsourced SOC. Does this replace it?

No, it works next to it. It can forward the logs your SOC needs in real time, and it answers the questions a SOC doesn't: what's in your directory, your Microsoft 365 tenant and your Group Policy.

Is it a product or a service?

Both: the software plus the work of connecting and tuning it.
